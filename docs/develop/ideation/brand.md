# Brand

The app is called Snuffle. Its brand so far is a name, a provisional mark, two typefaces and a neutral; the accent colours are still being explored. This page gathers the pieces and holds the naming work; each piece's exploration stays on its own board.

```summary
Takeaways:
- Snuffle sounds good, is playful and is about looking for things; it comes from a dog sniffing with its nose down. It matches the mark, a retriever with an awning for a mouth, and the concierge's job of finding one thing among many small shops.
- The agent and I brainstormed names; I asked it to research retriever names and words for sniffing and fetching.
Call:
- The app is Snuffle (decision 035). The docs site carries the name and the mark.
AI:
- An agent researched the two lines and ran quick conflict checks. I set the line (cute, childlike, about sniffing or fetching) and chose Snuffle.
Trade-offs:
- The conflict checks are quick web searches, not a trademark search; a UK IPO search and an App Store check come before any launch.
- A secondary sense of "snuffle" is breathing noisily with a cold. It is mild, and the dog sense is the common one around pets.
```

## Naming

The agent and I brainstormed names together. I asked it to research two lines: what people in the UK call their retrievers (Luna, Bailey, Buddy, Teddy, and Nous, the dog the Golden Retriever breed started from), and cute words for sniffing and fetching (Snuffle, Sniffari, Truffle, Rootle, Boop, Waggy). It ran quick web searches for conflicts and dropped words with a negative second meaning, such as Sniffy (disdainful) and Snoot (a snob). Earlier rounds had already ruled out Fetch, Rummage and Parade.

I chose Snuffle because it sounds good, it is playful, and it is about looking for things and finding them. It comes from the retriever: a dog snuffles when it works the ground with its nose, and a snuffle mat hides treats for a dog to find by smell, which is what the concierge does across a street of small shops. No app in the quick search used it.
