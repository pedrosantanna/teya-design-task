# Brand

The app is called Snuffle. Its brand so far is a name, a provisional mark, two typefaces and a neutral; the accent colours are still being explored. This page gathers the pieces and holds the naming work; each piece's exploration stays on its own board.

```summary
Takeaways:
- Snuffle is the sound and the motion of a dog sniffing with its nose down. It matches the mark, a retriever with an awning for a mouth, and the concierge's job of finding one thing among many small shops.
- The name came out of three rounds: the concept directions, retriever names, and words for sniffing and fetching, with quick conflict checks on each.
Call:
- The app is Snuffle (decision 035). The docs site carries the name and the mark.
AI:
- An agent proposed the name lists, ran quick web searches for conflicts and dropped words with a negative second meaning. I set the line (cute, childlike, about sniffing or fetching) and chose Snuffle.
Trade-offs:
- The conflict checks are quick web searches, not a trademark search; a UK IPO search and an App Store check come before any launch.
- A secondary sense of "snuffle" is breathing noisily with a cold. It is mild, and the dog sense is the common one around pets.
```

## Naming

I chose Snuffle. A dog snuffles when it works a patch of ground with its nose, and a snuffle mat hides treats in strips of fabric for a dog to find by smell, which is close to what the concierge does across a street of small shops. The word is cute and a little childlike, it belongs to the retriever in the mark, and nothing in the quick search used it for an app.

### Name ideas

| Direction | Names | What the quick search found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The terrier | Rummage, Fetch, Ferret, Terrier, Snout | Rumage, a UK search engine for second-hand goods, sounds the same. Fetch Rewards and Fetch Pet Insurance (US). eFerret, an eBay alert app. Terrier and Snout not checked |
| The Irregulars | Wiggins, Irregulars, Legwork, Runners, Footwork | No UK shopping app called Wiggins found. Netflix's The Irregulars (2021). Legwork is used by several companies, one of them an app that pays people to visit local shops. Runners and Footwork not checked |
| Nipping out | Popround, Nippy, Errand, Back in 5, Nip | Popround: nothing found. Nippy is a UK rapid grocery start-up. Errand is crowded. "Nip" is an ethnic slur against Japanese people. Back in 5 not checked |
| The parade | Parade, Barrow, Fascia, Awning, Coster | Parade is a UK-founded platform selling for independent skate shops, and a US underwear brand. Barrow is a place name with its own town app. The rest not checked |

### Retriever names

The mark became a retriever, so I looked at what people in the UK call their retrievers. The most common dog names in 2025 were Luna, Bella, Milo, Teddy and Poppy; for Golden Retrievers and Labradors, Bailey, Buddy, Luna, Cooper and Rosie come up most. The breed's own history adds a few: Nous, the yellow retriever Lord Tweedmouth bred from at Guisachan in 1868, and the first litter's puppies, Cowslip, Ada, Primrose and Crocus.

| Name | Where it comes from | What the quick search found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nous | Lord Tweedmouth's yellow retriever, the breed's founding dog. In British English "nous" also means common sense ("use your nous") | Nous is a London household-bills app (nous.co, founded 2021): a different market, but the same app stores and a UK audience |
| Bonnie | Scottish for pretty, the breed's home; in the top 10 Golden Retriever names in 2024 | No UK shopping app found. Bonny, a German receipt-rewards app, is close in spelling |
| Bailey | Top Labrador name in one survey, second for Golden Retrievers in another | No local-shops app found; a crowded word (Baileys liqueur) |
| Buddy | Top Golden Retriever name in one survey, top male Labrador name in another | No local-shops app found; very generic |
| Teddy | The UK's most common male dog name in 2024 and 2025 (Agria) | No local-shops app found; very generic |
| Primrose | One of the first litter's puppies; also a British flower and a London district | Not checked |

These are quick web searches, not trademark searches.

### Sniffing and fetching names

I wanted a cute, almost childlike word tied to sniffing, tracking or fetching, with no negative second meaning.

| Name | What it means | What the quick search found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snuffle | A dog sniffing about with its nose down. A snuffle mat hides treats in fabric strips for a dog to find by smell, which is close to finding one item across many small shops | No UK app or start-up found. A secondary sense is breathing noisily with a cold |
| Sniffari | Dog owners' word for a slow walk where the dog's nose sets the route (sniff + safari); the RSPCA uses it | No app found in the search |
| Truffle | Truffle dogs sniff out something rare and hidden | Crowded: a US restaurant-tracker app, a Serbian loyalty app, TruffleHunter (a UK truffle supplier). No UK local-shops app |
| Rootle | British: to dig or rummage with the snout | Not used by an app in the search; the word leans towards pigs |
| Boop | Touching a dog's nose, from internet dog slang | An old messaging app called Boop; nothing in shopping. The link to finding is weak |
| Waggy | A wagging tail | A Chinese couples' app (Waggy) and a pet community app (Waggi) |

Left out for a negative second meaning: Sniff and Sniffy (British "sniffy" means disdainful), Snoot (a snob), Nosey (prying), Hound (to harass), Mooch (to scrounge). Fetch and Rummage were already taken.

The agent's pick is Snuffle: it is the most childlike, it describes the search itself, and nothing in the search used it.
