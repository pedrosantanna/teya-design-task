# Value and revenue

What the product is worth to shops and how it makes money, written as hypotheses. The concept answers the shopper's and the shopkeeper's problems, and it also has to pay for itself. I work from one scenario: the concierge belongs to a payment provider with its own POS, as Teya is. The revenue comes from payments, from bringing shops onto the provider's network, and from promotion.

## Value to shops

The fees are what the provider earns. A shop judges the app by what it gets, and that is also what brings it onto the provider's POS and keeps it there (H13, H14).

- The app is an acquisition channel for the shop. It brings shoppers who wouldn't have walked in: someone at work asking for a part, someone two streets away who didn't know the shop stocks it. A small shop gets them without paying for ads or giving up a cut of the sale.
- The POS reaches past the counter. Today it sells only to whoever is in the shop. With the app, the same POS takes a request, a held item paid ahead or a delivery from a shopper who isn't there. The shop sells to the street without building a website or joining a delivery app.
- That gives a shop a reason to choose the POS and to keep it. A terminal is easy to swap for a cheaper one. A POS that brings shoppers is harder to leave, and easier to recommend to the shop next door.

## Network scenario

I treat this as a hypothetical scenario: the concierge is part of a POS provider's ecosystem. Two versions are possible.

- **Network only.** Only shops on the provider's POS are listed and answer requests. The app is a benefit of the POS, and a reason for other shops to join. The street looks thin until enough shops are on it.
- **Open, paid through the network.** Any shop can answer, and every payment goes through the provider. Coverage is better from the start, and joining the POS becomes the upgrade (faster payouts, requests answered from the POS itself).

For the proof of concept I start open, because the hypotheses need enough shops answering (H1), and I record which shops already use the provider's POS so both versions can be compared.

## Other sources I'd consider

- **A booking fee on delivery.** The shopper pays a flat fee when the concierge books a courier, with a margin on top of the courier's price. It depends on shoppers accepting a delivery fee for local shops (H7).
- **Paid demand insights.** The weekly demand summary is free; a paid version would add detail by hour, by item and against the rest of the street. It depends on shops valuing the free summary first (H9).

Both stay out of the hypotheses until the proof of concept says whether their preconditions hold.
