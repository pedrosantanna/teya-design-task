# The high street today

For most British people the high street is the main street of their town or neighbourhood: shops, cafés, a post office and a pub within walking distance of home, often with flats above them. In 2026 it has fewer shops and more cafés and services than it did ten years ago, and footfall is still falling, but empty units are being let again and the latest Budget cut business rates for the smallest shops.

## What a high street means

The Office for National Statistics counts a high street as any cluster of 15 or more retail addresses within 150 metres. By that measure Britain had 6,969 of them in 2019, and London had the most, 1,204 ([ONS, 2019](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates/articles/highstreetsingreatbritain/2019-06-06)). Today they are as much places to live as places to shop: more than half the addresses on Britain's central high streets are homes, about a third are shops and about one in ten are offices ([ONS, March 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/articles/highstreetsandretailareasingreatbritain/march2026)).

Historic England dates the decline to the 1980s, when car-based out-of-town shopping took trade away from town centres ([Historic England](https://cms.historicengland.org.uk/whats-new/features/historic-high-streets-quiz)). In 2004 and 2005 the New Economics Foundation coined "clone town" for high streets filled with the same chains, and counted 13 of 27 London high streets as clones ([Londonist, 2005](https://londonist.com/2005/06/attack_of_the_c)). That debate is where the idea of the high street as a mark of local identity comes from. After the 2008 crash, the [Portas Review](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-portas-review-the-future-of-our-high-streets) (2011) argued that high streets should become places for "shopping, learning, socialising and having fun". Bill Grimsey's reviews (2013, 2018, 2020) went further and said nostalgia would not save them ([Retail Tech Innovation Hub, 2018](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2018/7/4/grimsey-review-2-nostalgia-wont-save-the-high-street)).

The social side is measurable. In a 2017 study of London high streets, 45% of users said their main reason for being there was not shopping, 63% walked there, and almost 40% of the small businesses interviewed did something social, such as keeping an eye on older customers ([GLA / LSE Cities, *High Streets for All*](https://www.lse.ac.uk/asset-library/information/high-streets-for-all-report-web-final.pdf)).

## London and Hammersmith

London leans on its high streets more than the rest of the country. The GLA says 90% of Londoners live within ten minutes of one, and that high streets hold 41% of the city's businesses ([GLA](https://london.gov.uk/programmes-strategies/shaping-local-spaces/high-streets-all-join-high-street-network)). The ONS puts 45.4% of Londoners on or around a high street, against about one in five across Britain ([ONS, March 2026](https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/employmentandemployeetypes/articles/highstreetsandretailareasingreatbritain/march2026)).

**King Street** is Hammersmith's main shopping street ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Street,_Hammersmith)). Its anchors are national chains: M&S, a Sainsbury's Local, Boots, Superdrug, Lidl, and IKEA and H&M in Livat Hammersmith, the former Kings Mall that IKEA's property arm bought for £170m in 2020 ([M&S](https://www.marksandspencer.com/stores/hammersmith-1290); [Sainsbury's](https://stores.sainsburys.co.uk/4388/hammersmith-king-street-local); [Retail Gazette, 2020](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2020/01/ikea-buys-hammersmiths-kings-mall-launch-first-small-uk-store/)). The council's Civic Campus on King Street adds homes, a cinema, cafés and shops; contractor Ardmore went into administration in 2026 and Gail's was lined up for a unit ([Evening Standard via AOL, 2026-08-05](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/gails-bakery-set-open-major-100159000.html)). In January 2026 First Bus's London managing director called King Street "one of the worst bus bottlenecks in London" ([HammersmithToday](https://static.hammersmithtoday.co.uk/page/hammersmithtoday/info/cyclehighway054.htm)).

**Hammersmith Broadway** is the shopping centre and transport interchange over the Tube station. It hosts Made in H&F, a pop-up for local makers run by the council, the BID and partners, which the BID says has raised over £100,000 for independent makers ([Hammersmith Broadway](https://www.hammersmithbroadway.co.uk/shopping/made-in-handf); [Hammersmith BID annual report](https://hammersmithbid.co.uk/the-last-12-months-in-review-our-annual-report/)).

**Hammersmith BID** won its fifth-term ballot in October 2025, with 68.51% turnout and about 92% voting yes, for a 2026–2031 term worth at least £5m ([LBHF](https://www.lbhf.gov.uk/councillors-and-democracy/elections/hammersmith-bid-notice-ballot-and-arrangements-ballot/hammersmith-bid-result-ballot); [HammersmithToday](https://static.hammersmithtoday.co.uk/page/hammersmithtoday/info/conbid019.htm); [Hammersmith BID](https://www.hammersmithbid.co.uk/)). In 2025 its safety officer made 1,870 visits to businesses, recovered £50,299 of stolen goods and logged 554 incidents through a shared alert app. Its Summer Festival drew over 90,000 people, and it runs a Privilege Card that gives discounts at local businesses ([Hammersmith BID annual report](https://hammersmithbid.co.uk/the-last-12-months-in-review-our-annual-report/)).

**Hammersmith Bridge** has been closed to motor traffic since 2019 and reopened to cyclists and pedestrians in April 2025 ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/london/news/hammersmith-bridge-has-finally-reopened-to-cyclists-after-a-3-million-project-042225)). At the end of June 2026 the council recommended a "phased repair" that keeps it open for people on foot and bike, and reports said cars might never return ([Wandsworth Council](https://www.wandsworth.gov.uk/news/news-june-2026/wandsworths-deep-disappointment-at-hammersmith-bridge-latest/); [ITV](https://www.itv.com/news/london/2026-07-01/hammersmith-bridge-may-never-reopen-to-cars-because-of-lack-of-money-for-repairs); r/london: ["Plans to reopen Hammersmith Bridge to cars and buses axed over £300m cost"](https://www.reddit.com/r/london/comments/1uke914/plans_to_reopen_hammersmith_bridge_to_cars_and/)). Wandsworth said the decision would "do further harm to residents and businesses". Surveys claiming that 70–75% of local businesses were hurt come from campaigners, and no independent study was found.

**The neighbours** are different in character:

- **Shepherd's Bush** has its market between Goldhawk Road and Uxbridge Road, under redevelopment by Yoo Capital, and Westfield London nearby. In November 2025 the council rejected a community bid to list the market as an Asset of Community Value ([Thames Gazette/LDRS](https://thamesgazette.azurewebsites.net/breaking-news/shepherds-bush-market-redevelopment-moves-forward-after-acv-bid-rejected)).
- **Askew Road** (W12) is described by the council as a link between Hammersmith and Shepherd's Bush with a "vibrant and diverse mix of independent businesses" and a new traders' association ([LBHF](https://lbhf.gov.uk/business/business-connects/business-events-and-networking/business-associations-hf/askew-road-business-association)).
- **Chiswick High Road**, over the border in Hounslow, has a long local story of independents closing over rents and rates. The latest count found, from a 2018 traders' task force, was about 20 empty shops ([ChiswickW4](https://www.chiswickw4.com/info/contaskforcereport001.htm)).

**Chains and independents.** There is no current figure for Hammersmith's mix. The council's town-centre page once gave 31.9% independent retail and 9.8% vacancy, but it carried 2013 rent data and now returns a 404 ([LBHF](https://www.lbhf.gov.uk/business/business-connects/thinking-moving-hf/our-town-centres)). What can be seen today is a chain-heavy King Street, with independents more visible on Askew Road, in Shepherd's Bush Market and on side streets.

## The rules that shape it

### Business rates

**What it is.** The property tax on shops and other non-domestic premises: the property's rateable value (roughly its annual rent) times a multiplier.

**How it works now.** Since 1 April 2026, retail, hospitality and leisure properties pay permanently lower multipliers instead of the old 40% relief: 38.2p for small ones (rateable value under about £51,000) and 43.0p up to £499,999. Every property worth £500,000 or more pays 50.8p, which lands on big stores and supermarkets. A revaluation took effect on the same day, based on rents at April 2024 ([GOV.UK multipliers 2026/27](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/22026-notification-of-non-domestic-rating-multipliers-for-202627/22026-notification-of-non-domestic-rating-multipliers-for-202627); [PwC](https://www.pwc.co.uk/press-room/press-releases/research-commentary/2025/uk-budget-2025--pwc-comments-on-business-rates-.html)). After a backlash over pub revaluations, pubs and music venues got 15% off from April ([Restaurant Online, 2026-01-27](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/01/27/government-confirms-pub-business-rates-support-package)).

**Status.** Chancellor John Healey's first Budget, on 28 October 2026, raised the Small Business Rate Relief threshold for qualifying retail, hospitality and leisure properties in England from £12,000 to £18,000 rateable value, with tapered relief up to £21,000. Government estimates that about 70,000 more businesses will pay no rates. Deloitte's summary gives the start date as 1 April 2027 and notes that vape shops are excluded; offices and workshops stay on the old thresholds ([Deloitte](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/measures-budget-2026/extension-of-small-business-rate-relief.aspx); [ITV, 2026-10-28](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-10-28/chancellor-john-healey-reveals-what-is-in-his-first-budget); [Talking Retail](https://www.talkingretail.com/news/industry-news/budget-reaction-business-rates-threshold-rise-a-major-win-for-local-shops-28-10-2026/)). Talking Retail reports it is paid for by making online marketplaces collect VAT at source. The ACS called it "a major step forward" and Bira "a real win for independent retailers". The BRC called it "smoke and mirrors": with rates "frozen in real terms", larger retailers face a 3.5% rise worth about £250m next April ([BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/corporate-affairs/2026/ungated/no-breathing-space-for-retail-as-budget-keeps-costs-climbing/)). The change had been trailed a week earlier (r/unitedkingdom: ["Pubs, cafes, and high street shops to be spared business rates in Budget overhaul"](https://www.reddit.com/r/unitedkingdom/comments/1x91emd/pubs_cafes_and_high_street_shops_to_be_spared/)).

**Why it matters.** A small bakery or florist with a rateable value under £18,000 should pay no rates from April 2027. Rent, wages and energy stay, and trade bodies named those as the bigger pressures.

### High Street Rental Auctions

**What it is.** A council power, from the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023, to auction a lease on a high-street shop that has stood empty.

**How it works.** If a unit has been vacant for at least 366 days in the last 24 months, the council can serve notice on the landlord and, if it isn't let voluntarily, auction a one-to-five-year lease ([GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/high-street-rental-auctions-non-statutory-guidance)).

**Status.** In force since 2 December 2024 ([SI 2024/1139](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/1139/made)). By July 2026 more than 40 councils were using it and over 60 properties were back in use, often without an auction. London had held none; Lambeth and Islington aim to by the end of 2026, and Hammersmith & Fulham is not in the GLA working group ([London Assembly briefing](https://gla.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s123120/4%20Appendix%202%20High%20Street%20Rental%20Auctions_Assembly%20Briefing%202026.pdf)).

**Why it matters.** It puts shorter, cheaper leases within reach of independents, and the threat alone seems to move landlords. Each unit takes months, so it changes supply over years.

### Use Class E

**What it is.** Since 1 September 2020, shops, cafés, restaurants, offices, gyms, clinics and nurseries share one planning class ([SI 2020/757](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2020/757/contents/made)).

**How it works.** A unit can switch between any of those uses without planning permission. A separate right lets some Class E premises become homes.

**Status.** In force.

**Why it matters.** It explains why so many shops have turned into cafés, clinics and gyms, and why some units leave retail for good.

### Business Improvement Districts

**What it is.** A business-led body funded by a compulsory levy, often 1–2% of rateable value, for up to five years ([Local Government Act 2003, Part 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/26/part/4)).

**How it works.** Local businesses vote; a BID needs a majority both by number of votes and by rateable value.

**Status.** Hammersmith's is in its fifth term (see above).

**Why it matters.** BIDs already organise events, safety networks and loyalty cards on the street, and they know every business on it.

### Community Right to Buy

**What it is.** The English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026 replaces the 2011 "right to bid" on assets of community value with a right of first refusal on valued local assets such as shops and pubs ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/english-devolution-bill-receives-royal-assent); [Locality](https://locality.org.uk/news/new-community-right-to-buy-introduced)).

**How it works.** A community group can register a valued asset and gets first refusal when it is sold.

**Status.** Royal Assent on 29 April 2026; commencement dates not confirmed.

**Why it matters.** A neighbourhood can keep a shop or pub it cares about in community hands. In Shepherd's Bush the older right was refused for the market.

### Retail crime law

**What it is.** The Crime and Policing Act 2026 creates a stand-alone offence of assaulting a retail worker and ends the rule that treated shoplifting under £200 as a low-level offence ([GOV.UK factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-act-2026-factsheets/crime-and-policing-act-2026-retail-crime-factsheet)).

**How it works.** Assaults on shop staff are prosecuted as their own offence, and low-value theft can go to the Crown Court.

**Status.** Royal Assent on 29 April 2026; some parts need commencement regulations.

**Why it matters.** Theft and abuse are a daily cost for small shops. Police recorded 507,086 shoplifting offences in England and Wales in the year to March 2026, down 4% from a record ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/shop-theft-falls-4-but-retailers-warn-crisis-remains-underreported/721658.article)).

### Cash access

**What it is.** FCA rules from 18 September 2024 that protect access to cash when bank branches and ATMs close ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/new-fca-rules-protect-access-cash-already-having-impact)).

**How it works.** LINK assesses each closure. If it finds a gap in withdrawing or depositing cash, banks must fill it with a banking hub, a deposit machine or a better Post Office. Businesses can ask for a review.

**Status.** Live. Lloyds Banking Group announced 95 branch closures in February 2026 and LINK named 15 places for new hubs ([STV](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/nearly-100-lloyds-halifax-and-bank-of-scotland-branches-to-close)). No law makes a shop accept cash or cards, and the government said in 2025 it had no plans to change that ([Irish News/PA](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uk/bank-of-england-will-monitor-cash-acceptance-on-ongoing-basis-ABTIVRHVDBJ6PO223QXJDCMXTQ/)).

**Why it matters.** Small shops still bank their takings, and every branch closure makes that harder.

### Consumer rights for click & collect and delivery

**What it is.** Three laws: the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024.

**How it works.** Goods bought online, including online orders collected in store, carry a 14-day right to cancel, with exceptions for perishable and personalised items ([SI 2013/3134](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents)). Goods stay at the trader's risk until the customer has them ([Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/contents)). Since 6 April 2025, mandatory fees must be in the headline price, fake reviews are banned and the CMA can fine directly ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/the-dmcc-act-consumer-elements-come-into-force-from-6-april-2025)). Food sold at a distance needs allergen information before purchase and again on delivery ([FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-industry/guidancenotes/food-sold-online)).

**Status.** All in force.

**Why it matters.** Anything that sells on a shop's behalf has to show the all-in price, say who the seller is, carry each shop's cancellation terms and hold allergen data for food.

## How small merchants get paid

Most small shops take cards on a terminal or a phone reader. In 2025, 64% of all UK payments were by card and 39% were contactless, and 65% of adults had a mobile wallet ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/news-and-insight/press-release/digital-payments-continue-grow-mobile-wallets-become-more-popular)). Cash still matters more in shops than elsewhere: it was 19.2% of retail transactions in 2024, against 8% of all payments, and about 1.4 million adults mainly use cash ([BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/corporate-affairs/2025/ungated/high-interest-rates-push-shoppers-from-credit-to-debit-cards/); UK Finance).

What public sources say merchants struggle with:

- **Fees and opacity.** Retailers paid £1.48bn in card fees in 2024 ([BRC](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/corporate-affairs/2025/ungated/high-interest-rates-push-shoppers-from-credit-to-debit-cards/)). The Payment Systems Regulator found Visa and Mastercard scheme and processing fees rose more than 25% in real terms between 2017 and 2023 and that fee information is confusing ([PSR](https://www.psr.org.uk/news-and-updates/latest-news/news/psr-finds-market-isn-t-working-well-for-card-scheme-and-processing-services/)).
- **Cash flow.** 45% of small firms said late payments had increased on a year earlier ([Fintech Times, GoCardless/FSB survey](https://thefintechtimes.com/gocardless-report-highlights-the-domino-effect-of-late-payments-and-calls-for-change/)). Every terminal provider above sells next-day settlement.
- **Banking cash.** Branch closures make it harder to deposit takings, which is why the FCA rules cover business deposits ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/new-fca-rules-protect-access-cash-already-having-impact)).

## What this means for the brief

*Interpretation. Each point is our reading of the evidence above, not a sourced fact.*

- **The shopper is probably on foot, so collection may matter more than delivery.** 90% of Londoners live within ten minutes of a high street and 63% of users walk to theirs. With Hammersmith Bridge closed to cars, shoppers from south of the river arrive on foot or by bike, or need delivery.
- **"What's available" has to cover more than shop stock.** Retail jobs on central high streets fell 19% while food and accommodation rose 18%, and Use Class E lets a shop become a café or clinic overnight. Food items also bring allergen rules with them.
- **Availability data has to cost the merchant almost nothing.** Few micro-businesses sold online when last measured, and their pressures are wages, energy, card fees and cash flow. Data the shop already produces, such as till and payment records, is a more realistic source than a catalogue someone has to type in.
- **The street already has an organiser.** Hammersmith BID runs a loyalty card, a safety network, events for 90,000 people and a makers' shop, and it knows every business on King Street. Working with it would be easier than recreating it.
- **The price shown has to be the price paid.** Drip-pricing rules and the 14-day cancellation right apply to anything bought at a distance, including click & collect, so fees, the seller's name and each shop's terms belong on screen before payment.
- **Cash and wallets will both be in play.** Cash is a fifth of retail transactions but most adults have a mobile wallet. Reserving in the app and paying at the counter would serve both groups.
- **Policy is moving in independents' favour, but visits are not.** Lower rates for the smallest shops and rental auctions should help small occupiers, yet high street footfall fell 3% in September 2026. Bringing people to the shop, or the shop to people, is the gap.
