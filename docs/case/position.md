# Problem and position

I chose findability, who has it right now, and let the transaction decide what an answer has to carry.

<!-- screenshot: the persona grid from the proto-personas page, with the stage where each post stopped -->

## The choice

The brief offers two problems: findability across hundreds of badly catalogued shops, and transaction, one basket across several shops resolving into one purchase. I put the choice off until after discovery (decision 001), so it would come from evidence, then chose findability (decision 017).

Of the 20 public posts I clustered, 8 stopped at the first step: finding out who has the item and whether the shop is open. People ask strangers on Reddit and WhatsApp, phone around or make wasted trips. Five stopped at the transaction, for reasons like opening hours and cash, and none was about buying from several shops at once. One basket across shops already exists online, with postal delivery.

Evidence: [Discover conclusion](../discover/conclusion.md), [proto-personas](../discover/personas/index.md).

## Who it's for

- Sam, the mission shopper (11 posts), needs a specific thing by a deadline. The prototype serves him first.
- Jess, the lapsed local (6 posts), works during shop hours. She comes second, buying ahead and collecting after work.
- Ruth, the regular (3 posts), keeps shopping locally while her shops close. I left her out.

## How the transaction shapes it

An answer only helps if the shopper can act on it in the same chat. So every answer carries the shop, the price, a collect-by date and Buy. The shopper isn't charged until the shop confirms it has set the item aside. One order is one shop, with collection as the default.

## What I assumed

The assumptions that carry the most risk are about shops: that they answer on WhatsApp in under ten minutes, and keep a bought item for about a week. No shopkeeper is in the evidence. Each assumption has a hypothesis that tests it.

Evidence: [concept](../define/index.md), [hypotheses](../define/hypotheses.md).

## What I left out

- An interface for shops: their side is states and WhatsApp messages.
- A basket across several shops.
- Delivery booked by Snuffle, real payments, real stock and real agents.
- Ruth, and interviews before building (decisions 015 and 020).
